Present Perfect Simple — when do we use it?

  • 1. A result by now — and the result can still change I have lost my keys. (I am looking for them now)
    She has broken her phone. (it doesn't work now)
  • 2. Experience by now — and your experience can still change Your life is not finished, so you can do it again I have been to Moscow twice. Have you ever sung in a choir?
  • 3. News and new information Our choir has won a competition! The technician has arrived.
There is always a connection with now.
I have lost my keys. → I can't open the door now. The situation is still true.
I lost my keys yesterday. → Past Simple: only a fact about yesterday. Maybe I found them — there is no connection with now.

The structure

+ affirmative
I / you / we / they + have (’ve) + V3I have seen this film.
he / she / it + has (’s) + V3My cousin has bought a guitar.
− negative
have not (haven't) + V3I haven't finished my homework.
has not (hasn't) + V3He hasn't come home.
? question
Have / Has + subject + V3 …?Have you seen the new film? Has she called you?
Wh- + have / has + subject + V3 …?What have you done today?
V3 is the third form — the last column of the table: go → went → gone, see → saw → seen, be → was → been.
Good news: regular verbs have the same V2 and V3: work → worked → worked, attend → attended → attended.
After have / has we never use V2: I have went ✗ → I have gone ✓.
