Grammar: Past Continuous

Past Continuous — when do we use it?

1. An unfinished action at a specific moment in the past

At 7 p.m. yesterday, I was watching a film.

When my friend called, I was working on a website.

A specific time or a Past Simple action tells us when the longer action was in progress.

2. Two simultaneous processes

While Anna was checking the tickets, I was speaking English with the guests.

Both actions were happening at the same time in the past.

Remember: Yesterday alone is not a specific moment. Compare: I worked yesterday (completed) and I was working at 6 p.m. yesterday (in progress then).

Indicators

at 5 o'clock yesterdayat that momentwhen + Past Simplewhile + Past Continuous

Form

StructureExample
+subject + was / were + verb-ingHe was working.
−subject + wasn't / weren't + verb-ingThey weren't talking.
?Was / Were + subject + verb-ing?Were you reading?
Wh-?Wh-word + was / were + subject + verb-ing?What was she doing?

Was: I, he, she, it. Were: you, we, they. Do not use did in Past Continuous questions or negatives.
