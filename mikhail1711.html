When do we use the Present Perfect Continuous?

Situation 1 · Action is still going onSituation 2 · Result now from a process
The action started in the past, is still happening now, and the period of time is mentioned. I have been studying physics for two hours. I started two hours ago and I am still studying now. We have a visible result now, and we name the process that has led to this result. I am tired because I have been doing a difficult analysis. Result now: I am tired. Process: doing a difficult analysis.
We have been sitting in this café since two o'clock. We are still in the café now, and we say how long. The courtyard is wet because it has been raining. Result now: the ground is wet. Process: raining.

Indicators (Signal words)

Period of timeStarting point & recent process
for a couple of hours · for three days · for a long time
all morning · all day · all week
How long…? (How long have you been waiting?) 		since morning · since seven o'clock · since 2024
recently · lately (I have been reading a lot recently.)

How we build it · The forms

+ Affirmative
I / You / We / They + have been + V-ingWe have been talking for a couple of hours.
He / She / It + has been + V-ingShe has been working on the analysis all morning.
− Negative
I / You / We / They + haven't been + V-ingPeople haven't been sitting in the courtyard recently.
He / She / It + hasn't been + V-ingHe hasn't been feeling well lately.
? Questions
(Question word) + have + I / you / we / they + been + V-ing?How long have you been living in Georgia?
(Question word) + has + he / she / it + been + V-ing?What has she been reading all afternoon?
Spelling of V-ing: write → writing (drop silent -e) · sit → sitting, run → running (double the consonant after a short stressed vowel) · study → studying, play → playing.
