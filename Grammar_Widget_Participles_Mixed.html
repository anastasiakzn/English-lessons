Block 1: Participles and Participle Clauses (Theory)

Participles are verb forms that can function as adjectives or help form continuous/perfect tenses. In advanced grammar, they are widely used to shorten sentences and create Participle Clauses.

1. Participle I (Present Participle) → V-ing

  • Form: verb + -ing (e.g., reading, playing, working)
  • Usage: Used for active meanings (the noun is doing the action) or continuous processes.
  • The crying baby needs milk. (The baby is crying - active).
  • The man standing by the door is my boss. (Who is standing - active).

2. Participle II (Past Participle) → V3 / -ed

  • Form: regular verbs + -ed, irregular verbs 3rd column (e.g., broken, written, played)
  • Usage: Used for passive meanings (the action is done to the noun) or completed actions.
  • The broken window was repaired. (The window was broken by someone - passive).
  • The book written by him became a bestseller. (Passive meaning).

3. Participle Clauses

Participle clauses provide information about the main clause (time, reason, condition) in a more concise way. The subject of the participle clause must usually be the same as the subject of the main clause.

  • Present Participle Clause (V-ing): Used when two actions happen at the same time, or one happens immediately after another. Also used to explain a reason (since/because).
    Walking down the street, I met an old friend. (While I was walking...)
    Not knowing what to do, she cried. (Because she didn't know...)
  • Perfect Participle Clause (Having + V3): Used to emphasize that one action was completely finished before the second action started.
    Having finished his homework, he went outside. (After he had finished...)
  • Passive Participle Clause (V3 / Having been + V3): Used for passive meanings.
    Shocked by the news, they couldn't speak. (Because they were shocked...)
    Having been told the truth, he felt relieved. (After he had been told...)
