Grammar: Past Continuous

When do we use Past Continuous?

1. An action in progress at a specific moment in the past

We use the Past Continuous for an unfinished action at a concrete moment in the past.

A specific time: I was checking the website at two o’clock yesterday.

Another action in the Past Simple: When my mother came home, I was doing homework.

The Past Simple action tells us when the longer action was in progress.

2. Two actions happening at the same time

We use the Past Continuous for two simultaneous processes in the past.

While my husband was cooking dinner, I was watching TV.

While the guide was speaking English, the tourists were taking photos.

Both actions were in progress at the same time.

Remember: “Yesterday” alone does not tell us the exact moment. Compare: I watched a film yesterday (a completed action) and I was watching a film at 8 p.m. yesterday (an action in progress at a specific time).

+ Affirmativesubject + was / were + verb-ingShe was working.
− Negativesubject + wasn't / weren't + verb-ingThey weren't sleeping.

Was: I, he, she, it. Were: you, we, they. When we make a negative or a question, we do not use did. Example: Were you checking? ✓
