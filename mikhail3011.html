Compare Present Perfect Simple (completed action / result / number) and Present Perfect Continuous (duration / ongoing process). We are not working with non-action verbs yet.

Two forms · Two different focuses

Present Perfect Simple

have / has + V3

We focus on a completed action, the result or how many things are finished.

I have completed three diagrams.

Three finished diagrams are ready.

She has measured the copper plate twice.

Two completed measurements.

Present Perfect Continuous

have / has + been + V-ing

We focus on an activity's duration, an ongoing process, or a recent process with a visible effect.

I have been drawing diagrams since 9 a.m.

I started at 9 and am still drawing.

She has been measuring the copper plate for an hour.

She is still working on it.

Decision: Is the task finished / is the number of finished results important? → Simple. Are we focusing on a process still happening or its duration? → Continuous. For and since are useful clues, but are not enough on their own; read the whole situation.

Quick contrast

Result: They have repaired the display. It works now.
Process: Their hands are dusty because they have been repairing the display.
Quantity: We have tested five components.
Ongoing: We have been testing components since morning, and we are still at work.
