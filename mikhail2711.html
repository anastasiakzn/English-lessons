Compare Present Perfect Simple (completed action / result / number) and Present Perfect Continuous (duration / ongoing process). We are not working with non-action verbs yet.

Two forms · Two different focuses

Present Perfect Simple

have / has + V3

We focus on a completed action, the result or how many things are finished.

I have completed three diagrams.

Three finished diagrams are ready.

She has measured the copper plate twice.

Two completed measurements.

Present Perfect Continuous

have / has + been + V-ing

We focus on an activity's duration, an ongoing process, or a recent process with a visible effect.

I have been drawing diagrams since 9 a.m.

I started at 9 and am still drawing.

She has been measuring the copper plate for an hour.

She is still working on it.

Decision: Is the task finished / is the number of finished results important? → Simple. Are we focusing on a process still happening or its duration? → Continuous. For and since are useful clues, but are not enough on their own; read the whole situation.

Quick contrast

Result: They have repaired the display. It works now.
Process: Their hands are dusty because they have been repairing the display.
Quantity: We have tested five components.
Ongoing: We have been testing components since morning, and we are still at work.

          A museum experiment, online

          An original practice text · three paragraphs (A–C)

          AAt a small science museum, a student team has completed a virtual tour about heat and different materials. The theme is everyday science, and the purpose is to make simple experiments easy to explore from home. Visitors can open images, follow short explanations and compare the results of two experiments. The tour is designed to support classroom science, not to replace practical lessons.

          BOne part of the tour shows how heat moves through different surfaces. During a practical experiment, the team placed a copper plate and a wooden plate under the same warm lamp for five minutes. They then measured the surface temperature of each plate and recorded the readings. The website uses images and diagrams based on these real observations. The students explain that a virtual image can show the results clearly, but cannot perform the physical experiment for the visitor.

          CMaking the website easy to use has been another demanding part of the project. The programmers have been comparing two versions of the tour for several weeks, and the work is still continuing. One version includes frequent animations; the other uses simpler images instead. Initial tests suggest that the simpler version loads more quickly, so the team plans to reduce unnecessary effects. However, they have not yet measured how long visitors spend exploring the finished tour.
