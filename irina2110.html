Reading Practice (2 Exercises)

The Spicy Date

💡 Read the story carefully. Then do the two exercises below.

Polina sells clothes on a marketplace, and she usually works until late. Last Friday an old acquaintance called her. "My friend Mark wants to meet you," she said. "He's funny, and he loves Mexican food!" Polina had a lot on her plate, but she agreed to go to dinner with him.

The restaurant was small and noisy. Mark ordered tacos, and then he smiled: "Are you brave enough to try the spiciest dish on the menu?" Polina doesn't like spicy food at all, but she didn't want to look boring. "Of course," she said.

After the first bite, her face suddenly went red, and then pale. Her eyes were full of tears, and she nearly fainted. The waiter ran to the kitchen and brought her a glass of milk at once.

"If I weren't so stubborn, I wouldn't have ordered that dish," Polina laughs now. "And if I hadn't eaten that pepper, my mouth wouldn't still hurt today!" But the story has a happy ending. Mark thought she was brave and very funny. If she had said no to that dinner, they wouldn't be a couple now.

Grammar: Mixed Conditionals

Grammar Theory

In a mixed conditional, the two parts of the sentence are about different times. We mix the 2nd and the 3rd conditional.

TypeIf-clauseMain clause
Past → Present
A past action has a result now.		If + Past Perfect
(had + V3)		would + V
(now)
Example: If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.
(I didn't study medicine in the past, so I am not a doctor today.)
Example: If we hadn't missed the plane yesterday, we would be in Italy now.
Present → Past
Something that is always true about a person explains a past result.		If + Past Simple
(I / he / she were…)		would have + V3
(in the past)
Example: If I were braver, I would have asked him for his number yesterday.
(I am not a brave person, so yesterday I didn't ask.)
Example: If she spoke English, she would have got that job last year.
Time words help!
now, today, still → present
yesterday, last year, in 2020 → past
Compare:
3rd: If I had studied, I would have passed the exam. (past → past)
Mixed: If I had studied, I would have a diploma now. (past → now)
