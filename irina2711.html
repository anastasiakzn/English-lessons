Grammar: Past Perfect Simple vs Past Perfect Continuous

When do we use each tense?

Past Perfect Simple

Finished before a past moment

Use it when the key information is what had been completed before another action or time in the past. It often focuses on the result or how many.

By the time the guests arrived, we had prepared all the food.
She had answered 20 emails before lunch.
Past Perfect Continuous

Activity or duration before a past moment

Use it when the key information is how long an activity had been happening before another past action, or when that activity explains a past result.

We had been preparing food for two hours when the guests arrived.
She was tired because she had been answering emails all morning.

Quick structure reminder

Ask yourself: Is the speaker interested in the finished result / number, or in the activity / duration / reason for a past result?
