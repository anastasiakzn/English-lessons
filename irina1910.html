Last Friday I had a lot on my plate: twenty orders to pack, a meeting with a supplier and my daughter's school concert. For a moment I just lay there and thought: "Why me?" Then a man stopped and helped me up. He looked familiar: it was Dima, an old acquaintance from university! I hadn't seen him for ten years. Grammar: Mixed Conditionals

Grammar Theory

In a mixed conditional, the two parts of the sentence are about different times. We mix the 2nd and the 3rd conditional.

TypeIf-clauseMain clause
Past → Present
A past action has a result now.		If + Past Perfect
(had + V3)		would + V
(now)
Example: If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.
(I didn't study medicine in the past, so I am not a doctor today.)
Example: If we hadn't missed the plane yesterday, we would be in Italy now.
Present → Past
Something that is always true about a person explains a past result.		If + Past Simple
(I / he / she were…)		would have + V3
(in the past)
Example: If I were braver, I would have asked him for his number yesterday.
(I am not a brave person, so yesterday I didn't ask.)
Example: If she spoke English, she would have got that job last year.
Time words help!
now, today, still → present
yesterday, last year, in 2020 → past
Compare:
3rd: If I had studied, I would have passed the exam. (past → past)
Mixed: If I had studied, I would have a diploma now. (past → now)

1. Alina fell on the ice, so she knows Dima now.

2. I didn't sleep last night, so I'm tired now.
→ If I (sleep) last night, I wouldn't be tired now.

3. I'm not good at maths, so I made a mistake in the report.
→ If I (be) good at maths, I wouldn't have made a mistake in the report.

→ If she spoke English, she (get) the job.
would have got

→ If we had bought a warehouse last year, we (not / rent) one now.
wouldn't rent
